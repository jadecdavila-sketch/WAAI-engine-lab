The short version

What the Lab is, how much time it asks for, and who checks the work.

One framework

The Lab builds the products WAAI needs and teaches members by having them build. Counterpoint is the framework for both: the same Council, the same Journey, the same checks, whether the work is a three week experiment or a product members will use.

A human checks every piece of work

Every piece of work has a check, and a person runs it before the work is accepted. Nothing ships to members until the release checklist is signed. That, and the framework around it, is the Lab's answer to AI slop.

Three to five hours a week

One critique session a week, everything recorded, a showcase at every gate instead of a competition. Built for members with jobs, families, and businesses.

Teams of two to four

Small teams, formed at onboarding. Experiments are welcome. One review a week runs through the Council lenses, and anything that launches to members runs the whole framework.

The Counterpoint framework

Ten stages take a product from an idea to something people rely on. Three parts hold every stage to account: the North Star, the Council, and the Foundation.

The Journey

Ten stages from Discovery to Scale. Each has a question, a definition of done, and the Council voices that lead it. A gate at the end of each stage is a Council review held to the North Star. Inside the stages sit eighty nine activities, each naming who drives, what AI can carry, what a person must own, and how you know it was done right.

Module 3 walks all ten.

The Product North Star

Who the product is for, what it must do, and what it will not do, written plainly enough that an AI agent can tell when a decision breaks it.

No project opens a code editor without one.

The Council

Nine voices, each reading the work through one lens. In the Lab, members sit in those seats. AI critics run the same lenses the rest of the time, in the Lab and, through the Counterpoint plugin, on your own computer. The Council does not vote. It names the tensions and hands them to you.

Critique is structured, expected, and safe.

The Foundation

A living map of the codebase. Every person and every agent reads it before touching the code and updates it before leaving. A hook on the way out makes that binding. Drawn here as a road atlas of one real codebase, Larkin's. Hover or tap any marker.

The handoff document is always current.

The one rule you will meet in every module

A human is responsible for every activity, in every mode. Whoever drives, a person confirms the work passed its check before it is accepted.

Counterpoint is named for the musical idea: independent voices held together by rules, so that the tension between them makes something alive. The nine voices of the Council are the clearest case, and the same idea runs through the stages, the checks, and the documentation.

This course covers the Council, the North Star, with Counterpoint's own as the example, and the Journey. The Foundation waits for session two, when the plugin arrives and you can watch it write one. A preview is here, drawn as a road atlas.

How this course works

Best in order, but every module is open, so go where you like. The line at the top of each page shows where you are and takes you anywhere. Your answers are saved in this browser only, so you can leave and come back. Clear the browser and they go with it.

When you are ready, take the quiz. Twelve of fifteen gives you a completion code. Post it in the Lab channel and your seat in session two is held.
